Cosmology · Gravitational lensing · Dark matter

Manish Tamta

I study how gravity bends light and gravitational waves — and what those distortions reveal about dark matter and the Universe.

I’m a Ph.D. candidate in Physics at the Indian Institute of Science (IISc), Bangalore, advised by Nirmal Raj and Prateek Sharma. My work sits at the intersection of gravitational lensing, gravitational waves, dark matter, and cosmology, with a growing interest in machine learning for physical data. I’ve been a visiting researcher at the Max Planck Institute for Gravitational Physics (Albert Einstein Institute) in Potsdam, and I’m part of the LISA Consortium’s Cosmology working group.

Raised in the mountains of Uttarakhand. Off the clock you’ll find me travelling, birdwatching, and taking photographs.

01 — Research

Lensing as a probe of the dark Universe

Compact lenses — black holes, dense dark-matter structures — leave fingerprints on the light and gravitational waves that pass them. I build the wave-optics theory of those fingerprints and use them to search for dark matter across the electromagnetic and gravitational-wave spectrum.

Gravitational lensing & wave optics

When a signal’s wavelength is comparable to a lens’s Einstein radius, geometric optics fails and the lens diffracts. I compute the resulting frequency-dependent amplification — the observable that turns a lensed source into a measurement of the lens.

Dark matter

Primordial black holes and ultralight (“fuzzy”) dark matter both act as lenses. I map where microlensing and wave-optics signatures can open — or close — windows of dark-matter parameter space.

Multi-messenger astronomy

The same lens looks different to an X-ray telescope and to LISA. I connect microlensing of X-ray sources to strong and wave-optics lensing of gravitational waves, so observations at opposite ends of the spectrum constrain the same physics.

Increasingly, I use machine learning and deep learning to pull these faint lensing signatures out of astronomical and gravitational-wave data.
